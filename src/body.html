From scouting to application

Use the guide in the field

Start with the disease and field conditions, then match a product’s exact label. The steps below support a decision; they do not assign a field-specific risk score or an efficacy rating.

Recognize the disease

Look for initially water-soaked stem lesions that later turn pale or bleached, premature ripening, wilting or lodging. Damp conditions may produce cottony white growth. Hard, black, irregular sclerotia inside or on the stem strongly support a Sclerotinia diagnosis.

The fungus survives in sclerotia in soil and residue. Airborne spores can colonize petals; infected petals caught in the canopy then provide an infection route. Direct infection from soil-contacting fungal growth is also possible.

Source: Oklahoma State EPP-7095, with diagnostic photographs.

Scout before bloom, too

Tennessee confirmed white mold in winter canola in January 2026, likely through basal infection. Bloom guidance therefore does not mean plants are immune before flowering. Inspect wet low spots, dense canopies, shaded edges and fields with a disease history.

Blackleg, gray stem, freeze injury and mechanical damage can resemble parts of this syndrome. Harmless bird’s nest fungi can resemble the small fungal cups. Photograph the whole plant and split stem; ask a diagnostic laboratory to examine uncertain samples.

Source: University of Tennessee, February 2026.

Assess moisture and field history

Wet soil before flowering, persistent canopy moisture during bloom and dense growth favor disease. A dry interval can reduce risk, but a single weather observation cannot determine whether infection will occur. Consider forecast rain, irrigation, canopy drying, crop stage and previous disease together.

Use regional tools within their intended area. NDSU’s risk map and calculator use NDAWN weather and field information; the map applies to flowering canola. It is not a validated local forecast for every U.S. winter-canola region.

Background: Oklahoma State; regional tool: NDSU.

Stage the crop, then time the spray

Kentucky Extension describes a general protective window of 20–50% bloom; about 14–16 open flowers indicates 20% bloom. Use a crop staging reference and representative plants, rather than the percentage of the field that looks yellow.

Product directions differ: some specify 10–25% flowering, some 20–40%, and others 20–50%. Follow the disease-specific directions in the product profile and container label. A repeat spray must meet the interval, maximum number, annual total and harvest interval. Established stem damage is not reversed by a protective bloom spray.

Sources: Kentucky Extension, March 2026; each linked product label. Kansas State growth-stage illustration.

Build a spray plan from the exact formulation

  1. Identity: match product name, EPA registration, active concentrations and formulation. A shared brand name can refer to different registrations. Confirm that the canola/white-mold use appears on the labeling accompanying the product.
  2. Rate and capacity: keep fluid volume separate from dry weight. Record treated acres, rate, tank volume and calibrated gallons per acre. Check the disease-specific rate rather than a broad crop-table range.
  3. Method and coverage: check whether ground, air or chemigation is permitted for this crop. Follow that method’s minimum water volume, nozzle, droplet, boom, wind and buffer instructions. Do not assume the ground volume is also the aerial volume.
  4. Mixing: check each partner’s label, mixing order and adjuvant restrictions. Physical compatibility alone does not establish permission or biological compatibility. A biological’s living organism can require additional handling precautions.
  5. Access and harvest: confirm PPE, entry interval, harvest interval, grazing/feeding and rotational crop rules. A blank or “not stated” summary is not a waiver of the full label.
  6. Season totals: add earlier applications, relevant seed treatments and shared active ingredients from other brands. A premix counts toward each applicable active-ingredient maximum.

Each product’s details include links to relevant pages of its cited label. Topic links are text-search aids, not a substitute for reading the full label.

Manage resistance across the program

Changing brands can leave the same mode of action in use. Compare all FRAC groups in a premix, follow label alternation and consecutive-application limits, and use permitted partners that are effective against the target disease.

Reduce risk beyond the spray

Use locally appropriate stand density and nitrogen rates to avoid an excessively humid canopy. Where irrigation management permits, allow foliage to dry before nightfall. Nonhost grasses and cereals in rotation can reduce inoculum, but persistent sclerotia and other susceptible hosts mean rotation does not guarantee elimination.

Deep burial can move sclerotia away from the surface, while later tillage can return them. Choose a rotation and tillage plan with local agronomic advice. Soil-directed biological treatments are a different intervention from the foliar products summarized here.

Source: Pacific Northwest handbook, March 2026.

Interpret research in context

The Research evidence section includes trial methods, outcomes and limitations from U.S. and international sources. Disease reduction does not always produce a statistically significant yield benefit. Experimental rates, historical registrations and foreign labels are not application directions for this guide.

Efficacy fields remain blank. The evidence does not support a single universal winter-canola ranking. Use the full study to assess its relevance and the exact current container label to plan an application.

Location-specific requirements

This is a federal guide. Check state registration and any applicable supplemental labeling separately. An EPA master label can contain uses that a particular marketed brand label omits.

If the product label requires EPA Bulletins Live! Two, obtain the bulletin for the field, product and application month within the permitted six-month window. Keep the printable result and follow any additional restrictions. A bulletin does not replace state requirements.

Use the EPA registration number, not the establishment number. For a distributor number with three parts, EPA directs users to enter the first two parts in Bulletins Live! Two.

Keep a useful field record

Record field/location, crop and growth stage, scouting observations, weather/forecast, decision rationale, date and time, exact product and EPA number, rate and acres, application method and water volume, adjuvants/partners, prior seasonal totals, applicator, entry/harvest dates and relevant label/bulletin versions. After harvest, record disease observations and yield with untreated comparisons when available. These are planning prompts; required legal records vary by jurisdiction and product.

Match the exact use

  1. Product identity: use the full EPA registration number, including a distributor suffix when present. Confirm the formulation and the label accompanying your container.
  2. Current record: confirm state status for the intended application date; a product name or federal active status alone is insufficient. State databases differ in coverage and update timing.
  3. Crop and disease: check the canola/rapeseed crop group, white-mold use, geography and application method. Confirm whether winter-canola wording or regional restrictions affect the use.
  4. Supplemental requirements: obtain applicable Section 24(c) special local needs labels, Section 18 emergency exemptions, supplemental labeling and expiration dates. A linked federal master does not replace these documents.
  5. Site restrictions: follow required endangered-species bulletins, drift/buffer rules and state or tribal restrictions. The directory does not enumerate all tribal authorities.
  6. Keep a record: retain the label version, date checked, official source and any regulator confirmation with your application records.

What omission from this guide means

This guide requires documented paired crop/disease evidence for a profile. Omission is not a legal determination: EPA explains that FIFRA Section 2(ee) permits certain unlabeled-pest uses on labeled sites, subject to label restrictions, and states may impose additional limits. Such uses are not inferred here. Resolve them with the responsible regulator and qualified local adviser.
